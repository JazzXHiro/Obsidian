# Is it Important? How?

Video games at base are a dominant media of an entertainment. They are a beautiful median of art. Games tell countless stories through the medium of visual/sensual/sound medium. However, not everyone can fully enjoy them due to physical, sensory, or cognitive barriers. Accessibility ensures that everyone who is willing to or has an interest to can participate in gaming regardless of disability. The addressed topic is very important because -

- **Inclusivity** - Regardless of physical/mental ability everyone has a right to a good piece of entertainment.
- **Legal responsibility** - Accesibility is increasingly becoming part of  digital accessibility laws.
- **Expands audience** - Making games more accessible increases the potential user base and community engagement.
- **Artistic Integrity** - Games are art and storytelling. Limiting access means excluding people from experiencing cultural narratives.
- **Social Connection** - For many, games are also a way to connect with friends/family. Lack of accessibility can cause isolation.

# User group?

Accessibility features help different groups of players, such as:

1. **Players with Visual Impairments**
    
    - Colorblind players (need color-blind friendly modes).
        
    - Blind or low-vision players (screen readers, audio cues, haptic feedback).
        
2. **Players with Hearing Impairments**
    
    - Deaf or hard-of-hearing players (subtitles, captioning, visual sound indicators).
        
3. **Players with Physical/Mobility Impairments**
    
    - Those with limited motor control (customizable controls, adaptive controllers).
        
    - One-handed or alternative input gamers.
        
4. **Players with Cognitive/Neurodiverse Conditions**
    
    - Dyslexia-friendly text.
        
    - Adjustable game speed/difficulty.
        
    - Simplified UI and tutorials.
        
5. **Temporary or Situational Disabilities** (often overlooked)
    
    - Someone with a broken arm.
        
    - Noisy environment where sound can’t be heard.
        
    - Poor lighting conditions.
