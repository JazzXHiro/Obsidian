# 5.4 Flex & flex-wrap

These two lines work **together** to control **how child elements are laid out and what happens when space runs out**.

```css
display: flex;
flex-wrap: wrap;
```

---

## 1️⃣ `display: flex;`

### What it does

Turns the element into a **flex container**.

This means:

- Its **direct children** become **flex items**
    
- Items are laid out along a **main axis** (horizontal by default)
    

### Default behavior

```css
flex-direction: row;   /* left → right */
flex-wrap: nowrap;     /* everything in one line */
```

So without changing anything:

- Items try to stay on **one row**
    
- They may overflow or shrink
    

---

## 2️⃣ `flex-wrap: wrap;`

### What it does

Allows flex items to **wrap onto multiple lines** when there isn’t enough space.

In plain English:

> “If items don’t fit in one row, move the extra ones to the next line.”

---

## 🔍 Before vs After

### ❌ Without wrapping (default)

```css
display: flex;
```

Result:

- All items forced into one line
    
- Can overflow container
    
- Items may shrink too much
