# Risk Management

- **Definition**: A *risk* is a potential problem with two key traits:  
  1. **Uncertainty**: May or may not occur (no 100% certainty).  
  2. **Loss**: If the risk occurs in reality, undesirable results or losses will occur.

## What is Risk Management? 
Risk Management is a systematic process of recognizing, evaluating, and handling threats or risks that have an effect on the finances, capital, and overall operations of an organization. These risks can come from different areas, such as financial instability, legal issues, errors in strategic planning, accidents, and natural disasters.

**Goal**: Predict risks and implement effective solutions.

### Importance of Risk Management
Risk management is important because it helps organizations to prepare for unexpected circumstances that can vary from small issues to major crises. By actively understanding, evaluating, and planning for potential risks, organizations can protect their financial health, continued operation, and overall survival.

```
Let’s Understand why risk management important with an example. Suppose In a software development project, one of the key developers unexpectedly falls ill and is unable to contribute to the product for an extended period. One of the solution that organization may have , The team uses collaborative tools and procedures, such as shared work boards or project management software, to make sure that each member of the team is aware of all tasks and responsibilities, including those of their teammates. An organization must focus on providing resources to minimize the negative effects of possible events and maximize positive results in order to reduce risk effectively. Organizations can more effectively identify, assess, and mitigate major risks by implementing a consistent, systematic, and integrated approach to risk management.
```

## Risk Assessment

- **Definition**: Systematic evaluation of risks in activities, projects, or decisions.  
- **Purpose**:  
  - Identify, analyze, and evaluate risks.  
  - Determine mitigation/management strategies.  
- **Components**:  
  - **Risk Analysis**: "What could go wrong?"  
  - **Risk Evaluation**: "Should we be worried, and what actions are needed?"

### Objective of Risk Assessment

The objective of Risk Assessment is to rank the risks in terms of their harm inflicting potential. For risk assessment, initial every risk ought to be rated in 2 ways:

 - The chance of a risk coming back true (denoted as r)
 
 - The consequence of the issues related to that risk (denoted as s).

Based on these 2 factors, the priority of every risk is computed:
$$ p = r*s$$
Where p is the priority with which the danger should be handled, r is the likelihood of the danger changing into true, and s is the severity of harm caused by the danger changing into true.

### Risk Assessment Steps

1. **Identify Risks**  
   - Gather potential risks from stakeholders, historical data, and expert input.  

2. **Analyze Risks**  
   - Assess impact and likelihood of each risk.  

3. **Evaluate Risks**  
   - Prioritize risks based on severity and urgency.  

4. **Mitigate Risks**  
   - Develop strategies:  
     - Avoidance  
     - Impact reduction  
     - Risk transfer  
     - Acceptance (if within limits)  

5. **Monitor & Review**  
   - Continuously track risks and adjust mitigation strategies.  

### Risk Assessment Matrix

A risk assessment matrix is a tool used to evaluate and prioritize risks based on their likelihood and impact. It is typically represented as a grid, where the x-axis represents the probability of a risk occurring, and the y-axis represents the potential impact of the risk.

**How to Use It:**

- **Plot Risks:** Identify and plot each risk on the matrix according to its likelihood and impact.

- **Categorize Risks:** Risks are categorized into different levels, such as low, medium, or high, based on their position on the matrix.

- **Prioritize Actions:** The matrix helps prioritize which risks require immediate attention and which can be monitored over time. High likelihood and high impact risks are addressed first, while low likelihood and low impact risks may require less urgent responses.

## Risk Management Strategies

Strategies for handling identified risks during **Risk Response Planning**:

1. **Risk Avoidance**  
2. **Risk Reduction**  
3. **Risk Sharing**  
4. **Risk Transfer**  
5. **Contingency Planning**  

### 1. Risk Avoidance Strategy

- **Definition**: Eliminate risk by avoiding activities that could trigger it.  
- **Example**: Not entering a volatile foreign market to prevent financial loss.  

#### **Pros**:  
✅ Eliminates potential negative outcomes.  

#### **Cons**:  
❌ Forgoes potential opportunities.  

#### **When to Use**:  
- Risks **far outweigh** benefits.  
- Potential consequences are **catastrophic** (legal, financial, health).

#### **Pros of Risk Avoidance**  
✅ **Eliminates specific risks** entirely.  
✅ **Reduces uncertainties** and potential costs.  

#### **Cons of Risk Avoidance**  
❌ **Limits growth/profit opportunities**.  
❌ May lead to **stagnation** if overused.  

#### **Example**  
A tech company avoids storing user data to **prevent data breaches** and legal issues.

### 3. Risk Sharing

- **Definition**: Distribute risk among multiple parties (e.g., partnerships, outsourcing).  
- **Purpose**: Leverage external expertise and reduce individual exposure.  
- **Use Case**: Large/complex projects requiring shared responsibility.  

#### When to Use:  
- High-uncertainty **innovative projects**.  
- Need to **mitigate financial losses**.  
- Requires **specialized knowledge** to manage risks.  

**Example**: Joint ventures in R&D projects.  

#### **Pros of Risk Sharing**  
✅ **Distributes risk** across multiple parties.  
✅ **Utilizes shared resources** and expertise.  

#### **Cons of Risk Sharing**  
❌ Creates **dependencies** on partners.  
❌ May reduce **control** over project outcomes.  

#### **Example**  
A small tech firm partners with a larger company to co-develop software, **sharing financial risks** in development and launch.

### 5. Contingency Planning  

- **Definition**: Prepares **alternative action plans** for unforeseen risks.  
- **Purpose**: Ensures business continuity through **readiness** and **flexibility**.  
- **Components**:  
  - Recovery plans  
  - Backup operations  
  - Resource reserves

##### **When to Use Contingency Planning**  
- Critical for environments prone to **sudden changes** (natural disasters, economic shifts, tech failures).  

##### **Pros**  
✅ **Boosts resilience** against disruptions.  
✅ **Ensures business continuity** during crises.  

##### **Cons**  
❌ **Resource-intensive** to develop.  
❌ May seem "non-productive" if unused.  

##### **Example**  
A bank maintains **backup IT systems** and manual processes to ensure uninterrupted customer service during outages.  

## Risk Monitoring and Control

Risk monitoring and control begins at the start of projects when all potential and known risks are identified, and then just as importantly, continues throughout a project as those initial risks are continuously tracked while new risks are also identified as work continues, changes and progress.

### Risk Monitoring Activities  

#### **Reviewing Risk Triggers**  
- Check for **early signs** of risk occurrence.  

#### **Tracking Residual Risks**  
- Monitor risks **remaining after mitigation**.  

#### **Reassessing Risks**  
- Re-analyze and **re-prioritize** risks regularly.  

#### **Identifying New Risks**  
- Watch for **emerging risks** during the project.  

#### **Evaluating Risk Response**  
- Assess effectiveness of **mitigation/contingency plans**.  

#### **Updating Risk Register**  
- Log changes in **status**, **ownership**, or **strategies**.  

#### **Communicating Risk Status**  
- Share updates with **stakeholders** and **team**.

### Outputs for Monitoring & Controlling Project Risks  

#### **Work Performance Information**  
- Records of **occurred risks** and response needs.  
- Tracks:  
  - Schedule progress  
  - Deliverables status  
  - Costs  
  - Resource requirements  

#### **Organizational Process Assets Updates**  
- Updated **templates** (risk planning, risk breakdown structure).  
- **Lessons learned** from past issues.  

#### **Change Requests**  
- Modifications to project plans (e.g., budget, timeline, resources) for **emergency responses**.  

## Risk Response and Evaluation  

#### **Risk Response Planning**  
- Final step in risk management.  
- Responses developed for:  
  - **Controllable known risks**  
  - **Uncontrollable known risks**  
- **No response** for unknown risks.  

#### **Focus Areas**:  
1. **Controllable Known Risks**  
2. **Uncontrollable Known Risks**  

### Controllable Known Risks  

#### **Risk Prevention Approaches**  
1. **Risk Avoidance**  
   - Eliminate risk by **not undertaking** the risky activity/project.  

2. **Risk Mitigation**  
   - **Reduce probability/impact** of risk (not elimination).  
   - **Example**:  
     - Safety training/gear for construction workers → lowers injury risk.  
   - Some risks are **transferred** (not mitigated within the project).

### Uncontrollable Known Risks  

#### **Reactive Response Strategies**  
1. **Contingency Planning**  
   - Set aside a **contingency budget** for anticipated risks.  
   - *Example*: Allocating 2% extra budget for potential inventory loss.  

2. **Risk Acceptance**  
   - **No response** if:  
     - Loss is **trivial** (response cost > risk impact).  
     - Loss is **massive/uncontrollable** (no viable mitigation).  

## Risk Response Strategies (4Ts)  

1. **Terminate**  
   - Modify project plan to **eliminate** risk impact.  
   - If not possible, take **pre-emptive actions** to avoid.  

2. **Treat**  
   - **Reduce probability** of risk through early actions.  
   - May require:  
     - Extra resources  
     - Schedule adjustments  
     - Plan modifications  

3. **Tolerate**  
   - **No changes** to project plan.  
   - Accept risk when no viable strategies exist.  

4. **Transfer**  
   - **Outsource risk** to a third party (e.g., insurance, vendors).
