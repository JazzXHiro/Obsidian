# Inverse & its properties
### **Inverse of a Matrix**

The **inverse of a square matrix** $A$, denoted as $A^{-1}$, is a matrix such that:

$$A^{-1} = A^{-1} A = I_n$$

where $$I_n$$ is the identity matrix of order $n$.

A matrix $A$ is **invertible (nonsingular)** if and only if its **determinant is nonzero** $(det(A) \neq 0)$. Otherwise, the matrix is **singular** and does not have an inverse.

---

### **Formula for the Inverse of a Matrix**

For a square matrix $A$, the inverse is given by:

$A^{-1} = \frac{\text{adj}(A)}{\det(A)}$​

where:

- $\text{adj}(A)$ is the **adjoint (or adjugate) of $A$**.
- $\det(A)$ is the **determinant of $A$**.

This method is particularly useful for small matrices but is computationally expensive for large matrices.

---

### **Inverse of a 2×2 Matrix**

For a $2\times 2$ matrix:

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$$

its inverse is:

$$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det(A)} \begin{bmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{bmatrix}$$

provided that $\det(A) = ad - bc \neq 0$.

### **Properties of the Inverse of a Matrix**

1. **Uniqueness**: If $A$ is invertible, its inverse is unique.
    
2. **Inverse of a Product**: For two invertible matrices AAA and BBB:
    
    $$(AB)^{-1} = B^{-1} A^{-1}$$
    
    This means that the inverse of a product is the product of the inverses **in reverse order**.
    
3. **Inverse of a Transpose**:
    
    $$(A^T)^{-1} = (A^{-1})^T$$
    
    That is, taking the inverse of a transpose is the same as transposing the inverse.
    
4. **Inverse of an Inverse**:
    
    $$(A^{-1})^{-1} = A$$
    
    This means that applying the inverse operation twice returns the original matrix.
    
5. **Inverse of a Scalar Multiple**: If $k \neq 0$ is a scalar and $A$ is invertible:
    
    $$(kA)^{-1} = \frac{1}{k} A^{-1}$$
6. **Determinant of an Inverse**:
    
    $$\det(A^{-1}) = \frac{1}{\det(A)}$$​
    
    This shows that the determinant of the inverse is the reciprocal of the determinant.
    
7. **Inverse of an Identity Matrix**:
    
    $$I_n^{-1} = I_n$$
    
    Since the identity matrix does not change any vector it multiplies, its inverse is itself.

8. $$|A^{-1}| = |A|^{-1}$$


### **Finding the Inverse Using Row Reduction**

For larger matrices, the inverse can be found using **Gaussian elimination**:

1. Form the augmented matrix $[A | I]$, where $I$ is the identity matrix.
2. Use **row operations** to transform $A$ into $I$.
3. The transformed right side of the augmented matrix becomes $A^{-1}$.
   
### **Applications of the Inverse Matrix**

- **Solving Linear Systems**: If $Ax = b$, then:
    
    $$x = A^{-1} b$$
- **Cryptography**: Used in encryption and decryption algorithms.
  
- **Computer Graphics**: Transformations and scaling in 3D graphics.
  
- **Control Systems**: Stability analysis in engineering.
