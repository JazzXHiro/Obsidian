# Complex Matrices
### **1. Hermitian Matrix**

A **square complex matrix** $A$ is called **Hermitian** if it is equal to its **conjugate transpose**, i.e.,

$$A^H = A$$ 

where $A^H$ (also written as $A^\dagger$) is the **conjugate transpose** of $A$, meaning:

$$(A^H)_{ij} = \overline{A_{ji}}$$

In other words, $A$ satisfies:

$$a_{ij} = \overline{a_{ji}}$$

**Example of a Hermitian Matrix**

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & i \\ -i & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$

- Taking the **conjugate transpose**, $$A^H = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -i \\ i & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$ Since $A^H = A$, it is Hermitian.

###### Properties -
- $$\text{For Hermitian Matrix, } a_{ii} = \mathbb{R} \;\text{(Diagonal Elements)}$$

### **2. Skew-Hermitian Matrix**

A **square complex matrix** $A$ is called **Skew-Hermitian** if its **conjugate transpose is equal to its negative**, i.e.,

$$A^H = -A$$

This means that the elements satisfy:

$$a_{ij} = -\overline{a_{ji}}$$

Also, all **diagonal elements** of a Skew-Hermitian matrix must be **purely imaginary or zero**, because:

$$a_{ii} = -\overline{a_{ii}} \Rightarrow 2a_{ii} = 0 \Rightarrow a_{ii} \text{ is purely imaginary or } 0$$

✅ **Example of a Skew-Hermitian Matrix**

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2+i \\ -2-i & i \end{bmatrix}$$

- Taking the **conjugate transpose**, $$A^H = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -2+i \\ 2-i & -i \end{bmatrix}$$ Since $A^H = -A$, it is Skew-Hermitian.

###### Properties -
- $\text{For Skew Hermitian Matrix either } a_{ii} = 0 \; or \; a_{ii}=\text{Pure Imaginary}$

---

### **3. Unitary Matrix**

A **square complex matrix** $U$ is called **Unitary** if its **conjugate transpose is equal to its inverse**, i.e.,

$$U^H U = U U^H = I \text{ or } AA^{\theta} = A^{\theta}A = I$$

where II is the **identity matrix**.

###### Properties -
- $(A+B)^{\theta} = A^{\theta} + B^{\theta}$
- $(AB)^{\theta} = B^{\theta}A^{\theta}$

✅ **Example of a Unitary Matrix**

$$U = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i \\ i & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

- Taking the **conjugate transpose**, $$U^H = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -i \\ -i & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
    - Checking $U^H U$: $$U^H U = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -i \\ -i & 1 \end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i \\ i & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1+1 & i-i \\ -i+i & 1+1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = I$$ So, $U^H U = I$, meaning $U$ is Unitary.

###### Properties -
- If Modulus of the determinant of a Matrix A:
$$|\det A| = 1 \text{ even if} \det A = \pm{1}$$

---
### More Imp. Properties-
- $$A = \frac{1}{2}(A+A^{\theta})+\frac{1}{2}(A-A^{\theta})$$
where $\frac{1}{2}(A+A^{\theta})$ is Hermitian Matrix & $\frac{1}{2}(A-A^{\theta})$ is Skew Hermitian Matrix

### **Key Properties**

| Matrix Type                     | Condition                                           |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hermitian** ($A^H = A$)       | Eigenvalues are **real**                            |
| **Skew-Hermitian** ($A^H = -A$) | Eigenvalues are **purely imaginary or zero**        |
| **Unitary** ($U^H U = I$)       | **Preserves length** and **complex inner products** |
