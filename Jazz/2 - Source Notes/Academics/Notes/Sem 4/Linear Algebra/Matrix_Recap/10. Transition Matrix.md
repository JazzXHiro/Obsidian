# Transition Matrix

A **transition matrix** is a square matrix used to describe the transitions of a **Markov chain**. It represents the probabilities of moving from one state to another in a system where the next state depends only on the current state.

### **Structure of a Transition Matrix**

If a system has nn states, the transition matrix PP is an n×nn \times n matrix, where each element PijP_{ij} represents the probability of transitioning from state ii to state jj:

P=[P11P12…P1nP21P22…P2n⋮⋮⋱⋮Pn1Pn2…Pnn]P = \begin{bmatrix} P_{11} & P_{12} & \dots & P_{1n} \\ P_{21} & P_{22} & \dots & P_{2n} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ P_{n1} & P_{n2} & \dots & P_{nn} \end{bmatrix}

### **Properties of a Transition Matrix**

1. **Non-Negative Entries:** All elements Pij≥0P_{ij} \geq 0, as they represent probabilities.
    
2. **Row Stochastic:** The sum of each row must equal 1:
    
    ∑j=1nPij=1,for all i\sum_{j=1}^{n} P_{ij} = 1, \quad \text{for all } i

### **Example**

Suppose we have a simple weather model with 3 states: **Sunny (S), Cloudy (C), Rainy (R)**. The transition probabilities are:

- If it's **Sunny**, there's a **70%** chance it stays Sunny, **20%** chance it becomes Cloudy, and **10%** chance it becomes Rainy.
- If it's **Cloudy**, there's a **30%** chance it becomes Sunny, **40%** chance it stays Cloudy, and **30%** chance it becomes Rainy.
- If it's **Rainy**, there's a **20%** chance it becomes Sunny, **50%** chance it becomes Cloudy, and **30%** chance it stays Rainy.

The transition matrix is:

P=[0.70.20.10.30.40.30.20.50.3]P = \begin{bmatrix} 0.7 & 0.2 & 0.1 \\ 0.3 & 0.4 & 0.3 \\ 0.2 & 0.5 & 0.3 \end{bmatrix}

### **Usage of Transition Matrices**

- **Markov Chains**: Modeling probabilistic state changes.
- **Game AI**: NPC behavior decision-making.
- **Economics**: Predicting market trends.
- **Robotics**: Motion planning and reinforcement learning.
