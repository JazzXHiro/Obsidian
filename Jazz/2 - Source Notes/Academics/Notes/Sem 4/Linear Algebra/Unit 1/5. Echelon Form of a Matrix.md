# Echelon Form of a Matrix

The echelon form of a matrix is defined by three key conditions:  
1. **Zero rows at the bottom**: All rows with only zeros are placed below non-zero rows.  
2. **Strictly increasing leading zeros**: The first non-zero element in each row (called the "leading entry") must be farther to the right than the one above it.  
3. **Leading entries as 1**: The first non-zero element in every row must be unity (1).  

To determine the **rank** of a matrix:  
1. Convert the matrix to echelon form using row operations (swapping rows, scaling rows to create leading 1s, and adding/subtracting rows).  
2. Count the number of non-zero rows in the resulting echelon form.  

**Example**:  
For the matrix  

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 3 & -2 & 6 \\ 
0 & 1 & 7 & 0 \\ 
0 & 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 
\end{bmatrix},$$

- It satisfies all echelon conditions.  
- The rank is **3** (three non-zero rows).  

**Key Steps to Echelon Form**:  
1. Swap rows to bring non-zero rows above zero rows.  
2. Create leading 1s by scaling rows.  
3. Eliminate entries below leading 1s to ensure each leading 1 is strictly to the right of the one above.  

This structured form simplifies solving systems of equations and determining matrix properties like rank.
