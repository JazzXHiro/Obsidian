# Visualization of System of linear equations

### **Visualization of a System of Linear Equations**

A **system of linear equations** consists of multiple linear equations that we want to solve simultaneously. The number of equations and variables determines how the system can be visualized.

---

### **1. System with Two Variables (2D Visualization)**

A system of two equations with two unknowns:

$$a_1x + b_1y = c_1$$$$a_2x + b_2y = c_2$$

represents two **straight lines** in the **xy-plane**. The solution to the system corresponds to the point(s) where the lines intersect.

**Possible Cases:**

1. **One Unique Solution** (Intersection at a Single Point)
    
    - The lines intersect at exactly one point.
    - The system is **consistent and independent**.

2. **No Solution** (Parallel Lines)
    
    - The lines never intersect.
    - The system is **inconsistent**.

3. **Infinitely Many Solutions** (Coincident Lines)
    
    - The lines lie on top of each other.
    - The system is **dependent**.

---

### **2. System with Three Variables (3D Visualization)**

A system of three equations with three unknowns:

$$a_1x + b_1y + c_1z = d_1$$ $$a_2x + b_2y + c_2z = d_2$$ $$a_3x + b_3y + c_3z = d_3$$

represents three **planes** in three-dimensional space. The solution is the point (or set of points) where these planes intersect.

**Possible Cases:**

1. **One Unique Solution** (Intersection at a Single Point)
    
    - The three planes meet at a single point.

2. **No Solution** (Parallel or Skew Planes)
    
    - The planes do not share a common intersection.

3. **Infinitely Many Solutions** (Intersection Along a Line or Plane)
    
    - The three planes intersect along a line.
    - The three equations may define the same plane.
