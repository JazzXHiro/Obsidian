# Elimination using matrices

### **Elimination Method Using Matrices (Gaussian Elimination)**

The **elimination method using matrices** is also known as **Gaussian elimination**. It is used to solve systems of linear equations by converting the system into an upper triangular form and then performing **back-substitution**.

---

### **Steps for Gaussian Elimination**

1. **Convert the system of equations into an augmented matrix**
    
    - The augmented matrix is written as $[A | B],$ where $A$ is the coefficient matrix, and $B$ is the column of constants.
2. **Use row operations to convert the matrix into row echelon form (upper triangular matrix)**
    
    - Swap rows if necessary.
    - Multiply rows by scalars to get leading coefficients as 1.
    - Subtract multiples of one row from another to eliminate variables.
3. **Perform back-substitution**
    
    - Once the matrix is in upper triangular form, solve for variables starting from the last equation.

---

### **Example: Solve Using Gaussian Elimination**

#### **Given system of equations:**

$$2x + y - z = 8$$ $$-3x - y + 2z = -11$$ $$-2x + y + 2z = -3$$

#### **Step 1: Write as an Augmented Matrix**

$$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & -1 & | 8 \\ -3 & -1 & 2 & | -11 \\ -2 & 1 & 2 & | -3 \end{bmatrix}$$

#### **Step 2: Convert to Upper Triangular Form**

- Make the first pivot (top-left element) **1** by dividing Row 1 by 2.
- Eliminate the first column below the pivot by row operations.
- Repeat the process for the next pivot (make the second leading coefficient 1 and eliminate below).
- Continue until you get an upper triangular matrix.

#### **Step 3: Perform Back-Substitution**

- Start solving from the last equation upwards.
