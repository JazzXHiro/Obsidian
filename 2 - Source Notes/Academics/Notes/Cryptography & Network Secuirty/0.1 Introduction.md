# 0.1 Introduction

- **When was Cryptography first used?**
		As early as Hieroglyphics

- ##### 🔐 What is a Scytale?

	A **Scytale** is a **cylindrical tool** used to perform a **transposition cipher**, which rearranges the letters of a message rather than substituting them.
	
	###### 🧠 How it Works:
	
	1. **Sender and receiver** both have identical **cylinders (rods) of the same diameter**.
	    
	2. The sender wraps a strip of parchment or leather (called a **ribbon**) around the cylinder.
	    
	3. The message is written across the **length** of the cylinder.
	    
	4. Once unwrapped, the letters appear **scrambled** and **illegible**.
	    
	5. To **decode**, the receiver wraps the ribbon around their own scytale and reads the message across the lines.

-  **Julius Caesar**
	  
	  Julius Caesar is significant to **cryptography and network security** because he is credited with one of the **earliest known forms of encryption** — now called the **Caesar Cipher**.
	  
	###### 🏛️ Historical Context
	
	Julius Caesar (100–44 BCE), a Roman general and statesman, used a **simple substitution cipher** to protect his military messages. This cipher became known as the **Caesar Cipher**.
	
	###### 🔐 What is the Caesar Cipher?
	
	The **Caesar Cipher** is a type of **substitution cipher**, where each letter in the plaintext is shifted a fixed number of places **down** the alphabet.
	
	- For example, with a **shift of 3**:
	    
	    - A → D
	        
	    - B → E
	        
	    - C → F
	        
	    - ...
	        
	    - X → A
	        
	    - Y → B
	        
	    - Z → C
	        
	
	So, the word **"HELLO"** becomes **"KHOOR"**.
