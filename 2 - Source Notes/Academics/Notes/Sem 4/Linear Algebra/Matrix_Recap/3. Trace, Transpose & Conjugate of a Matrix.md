# Trace, Transpose & Conjugate of a Matrix

### 1. **Trace of a Matrix**

The **trace** of a square matrix $A$ (denoted as $\text{tr}(A)$) is the sum of the elements on the principal diagonal of the matrix. The main diagonal consists of elements where the row and column indices are the same, i.e., $A_{ii}$.
$$
Tr(A) = \sum^{n}_{i=j}a_{ij}(i=j)
$$

For example, for the matrix $A$:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 9 \end{pmatrix}$$

The trace of $A$ is:

$$\text{tr}(A) = 1 + 5 + 9 = 15$$

#### Properties of Trace:

- **Linearity**: $\text{tr}(A + B) = \text{tr}(A) + \text{tr}(B)$
- **Scalar Multiplication**: $\text{tr}(kA) = k \cdot \text{tr}(A)$
- **Invariant under Transposition**: $\text{tr}(A^T) = \text{tr}(A)$
- **Trace of a Product**: $\text{tr}(AB) = \text{tr}(BA)$ (if $A$ and $B$ are square matrices of the same size)

---

### 2. **Transpose of a Matrix**

The **transpose** of a matrix $A$ (denoted $A^T$) is a new matrix obtained by swapping the rows and columns of $A$. Specifically, the element at position $(i, j)$ in $A$ becomes the element at position $(j, i)$ in $A^T$.
$$
\text{If } A=[a_{ij}]_{m\times n},\;A^{T} = [a_{ji}]_{n\times m}
$$
For example, if:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$$

Then the transpose of AA is:

$A^T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$

#### Properties of Transpose:

- **Double Transpose**: $(A^T)^T = A$ or $(A')' = A$
- **Transpose of a Sum**: $(A + B)^T = A^T + B^T$ or $(A+B)' = A' + B'$
- **Transpose of a Product**: $(AB)^T = B^T A^T$ or $(AB)' = (B'A')$
- **Transpose of a Scalar Multiple**: $(kA)^T = kA^T$ or $(kA)'=kA'$

---

### 3. **Conjugate of a Matrix**

The **conjugate** (or **complex conjugate**) of a matrix $A$, denoted as $\overline{A}$, is obtained by taking the complex conjugate of each element of $A$. The complex conjugate of a complex number involves changing the sign of the imaginary part, while the real part remains unchanged.

For example, if:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 + 2i & 3 - i \\ 4 + 5i & 6 \end{pmatrix}$$

Then the conjugate of $A$ is:

$\overline{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - 2i & 3 + i \\ 4 - 5i & 6 \end{pmatrix}$

#### Properties of the Conjugate:

- **Conjugate of a Sum**: $\overline{A + B} = \overline{A} + \overline{B}$
- **Conjugate of a Scalar Multiple**: $\overline{kA} = k\overline{A}$ (for scalar $k$)
- **Conjugate of a Product**: $\overline{AB} = \overline{A}\ \overline{B}$
- **Conjugate of a Transpose**: $\overline{A^T} = (\overline{A})^T$
- **Conjugate of a Real Matrix**: $\overline{A}=A \; \text{(For}\; \mathbb{R} \; \text{Matrix)}$
- **Conjugate of a Pure Imaginary Matrix**: $\overline{A}=-A \; \text{(For Pure Imaginary Matrix)}$

---

### 4. **Transpose Conjugate of a Matrix**
The **transpose conjugate** (also called the **adjoint**) of a matrix $A$ is a matrix operation that involves both taking the transpose and the conjugate of each element in the matrix. It is denoted as $A^H$ or $A^*.$

To compute the **transpose conjugate** of a matrix $A$, you first take the **transpose** of $A$ (swap rows and columns), and then take the **complex conjugate** of each element in the resulting matrix.

Mathematically, the transpose conjugate of $A$ is:

$$A^H = \overline{A^T}$$

where $\overline{A^T}$ represents the matrix obtained by taking the complex conjugate of the transpose of $A$.

### Example:

Let $A$ be the matrix:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 + 2i & 3 - i \\ 4 + 5i & 6 \end{pmatrix}$$

1. **Transpose of $A$**:

$$A^T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 + 2i & 4 + 5i \\ 3 - i & 6 \end{pmatrix}$$

2. **Complex Conjugate of $A^T$**:

$$A^H = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - 2i & 4 - 5i \\ 3 + i & 6 \end{pmatrix}$$

So, the **transpose conjugate** of $A$ is:

$$A^H = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - 2i & 4 - 5i \\ 3 + i & 6 \end{pmatrix}$$

### Properties of Transpose Conjugate:

- **Double Transpose Conjugate**: $(A^H)^H = A$
- **Transpose Conjugate of a Sum**: $(A + B)^H = A^H + B^H$
- **Transpose Conjugate of a Product**: $(AB)^H = B^H A^H$
- **Transpose Conjugate of a Scalar Multiple**: $(kA)^H = \overline{k} A^H$ (where $k$ is a scalar, and $\overline{k}$ is its complex conjugate)
- **Self-adjoint (Hermitian) Matrix**: If $A = A^H$, the matrix is said to be **Hermitian**.
- **Unitary Matrix**: A matrix $A$ is unitary if $A^H A = I$, where $I$ is the identity matrix.
