# Connectivity Devices & Cable Types

**Study Notes: Transmission Media**

---

### **Transmission Media Overview**  
- **Definition**: Communication channels that carry information from sender to receiver using electromagnetic signals.  
- **Types**:  
  - **Guided (Wired)**: Signals travel through physical media (e.g., copper, fiber).  
  - **Unguided (Wireless)**: Signals propagate through air/vacuum (e.g., radio waves, microwaves).  
- **OSI Layer**: Physical Layer (Layer 1).  

---
**Study Notes: Factors for Designing Transmission Media & Causes of Transmission Impairment**  

---

### **Factors to Consider for Designing Transmission Media**  

| **Factor**              | **Description**                                                                                     | **Example/Impact**                                                                 |  
|-------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|  
| **Bandwidth**           | The maximum data transfer rate supported by the medium. Higher bandwidth = higher data transmission speed. | Fiber optics (high bandwidth) vs. UTP (lower bandwidth).                         |  
| **Transmission Impairment** | Degradation of signal quality during transmission. Affects reliability and accuracy.               | Attenuation (signal loss) limits cable length.                                    |  
| **Interference**         | Unwanted signals disrupt the original signal. Can be electromagnetic (EMI) or cross-talk.          | UTP cables are prone to EMI; STP cables reduce interference.                      |  
| **Cost**                | Includes installation, maintenance, and material costs.                                            | Fiber optics are expensive to install but cost-effective long-term for high speed.|  
| **Distance**            | Signal strength degrades over distance. Affects choice of medium.                                  | Fiber optics for long distances; UTP for short LANs.                              |  
| **Ease of Installation**| Complexity of deploying the medium.                                                                | UTP is easy to install; fiber optics require specialized tools.                   |  
| **Environmental Factors**| Susceptibility to physical damage, temperature, and moisture.                                      | Coaxial cables are durable; fiber optics are fragile.                             |  

---

### **Causes of Transmission Impairment**  

#### **Attenuation**  
- **Definition**: Loss of signal strength over distance.  
- **Causes**: Resistance in the medium (e.g., copper wires).  
- **Impact**: Limits maximum cable length.  
- **Solution**: Use **repeaters** or **amplifiers** to regenerate signals.  

#### **Distortion**  
- **Definition**: Change in signal shape due to varying propagation speeds of frequency components.  
- **Types**:  
  - **Delay Distortion**: High-frequency components arrive later than low-frequency ones.  
  - **Harmonic Distortion**: Non-linear amplification alters signal waveform.  
- **Impact**: Errors in data interpretation (e.g., voice call static).  

#### **Noise**  
- **Definition**: Unwanted signals added to the original signal.  
- **Types**:

| **Type**                  | **Description**                              | **Example**                        |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Thermal Noise**         | Random electron motion due to temperature.   | Present in all electronic devices. |
| **Crosstalk**             | Signal interference between adjacent cables. | UTP cables in a bundle.            |
| **Impulse Noise**         | Sudden, high-energy disturbances (spikes).   | Lightning strikes, power surges.   |
| **Intermodulation Noise** | Mixing of signals at different frequencies.  | Poorly shielded equipment.         |

---

**Example Scenario**:  
- **Choosing UTP for an Office LAN**:  
  - **Pros**: Cheap, easy to install.  
  - **Cons**: Limited to 100 meters due to attenuation; requires shielding (STP) in high-EMI environments.
### **2. Guided (Wired) Media**  

#### **Twisted Pair Cable**  
- **Structure**: Two insulated copper wires twisted to reduce electromagnetic interference (EMI).  
  - **UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair)**:  
    - Common in LANs/telecom.  
    - Categories (Cat 1–Cat 5) support up to 200 Mbps:
	    - Category 1: Category 1 is used for telephone lines that have low-speed data. 
	    - Category 2: It can support upto 4Mbps. 
	    - Category 3: It can support upto 16Mbps. 
	    - Category 4: It can support upto 20Mbps. Therefore, it can be used for long-distance communication. 
	    - Category 5: It can support upto 200Mbps. 
    - **Advantages**: Cheap, easy installation.  
    - **Disadvantages**: Short-distance due to attenuation.  
  - **STP (Shielded Twisted Pair)**:  
    - Mesh shielding for higher data rates.  
    - **Advantages**: Reduced EMI, higher capacity.  
    - **Disadvantages**: Costlier than UTP, higher attenuation.

#### **Coaxial Cable**  
- **Structure**: Inner copper conductor + insulating layer + copper mesh shield.
- The name of the cable is **coaxial**l as it contains two conductors parallel to each other
- The inner conductor of the coaxial cable is made up of copper, and the outer conductor is made up of copper mesh. The middle core is made up of nonconductive cover that separates the inner conductor from the outer conductor.
- **The middle core is responsible for the data transferring whereas the copper mesh prevents from the EMI(Electromagnetic interference).**
  - **Types**:  
    - **Baseband**: Single signal at high speed (e.g., Ethernet).  
    - **Broadband**: Multiple signals (e.g., TV cable).  
  - **Advantages**: High bandwidth, better shielding than UTP.  
  - **Disadvantages**: Expensive, entire network fails if cable breaks.

#### **Fiber Optic Cable**  
- **Structure**: Core (glass/plastic) + cladding (reflective layer) + protective jacket.
- Fiber optic is a cable that holds the optical fibers coated in plastic that are used to send the data by pulses of light
  - **Types**:  
    - **Single-mode**: Thin core, long-distance (e.g., internet backbone).  
    - **Multimode**: Thicker core, shorter distances (e.g., LANs).  
  - **Advantages**:  
    - High bandwidth (up to terabits), immune to EMI.  
    - Long-distance (low attenuation), secure.  
  - **Disadvantages**: Expensive installation, fragile.  


##### **Basic Elements of Fiber Optic Cable**  
A fiber optic cable consists of three primary components:  

| **Element**  | **Description**                                                                                                                                                                                                      |     |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
| **Core**     | - Narrow strand of glass or plastic. <br> - Directs light pulses (data transmission). <br> - Larger core diameter allows more light transmission.                                                                    |     |
| **Cladding** | - Concentric layer surrounding the core. <br> - Made of glass with a **lower refractive index** than the core to reflect light back into the core. <br> - Ensures minimal signal loss via total internal reflection. |     |
| **Jacket**   | - Outer protective layer (plastic). <br> - Protects the core/cladding from physical damage (e.g., moisture, abrasion). <br> - Adds tensile strength.                                                                 |     |


##### **Advantages of Fiber Optic Cable Over Copper**  

| **Advantage**         | **Description**                                                                                                                      |     |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- |
| **Greater Bandwidth** | - Supports terabits/second speeds vs. copper’s gigabits. <br> - Ideal for high-data applications (e.g., streaming, cloud computing). |     |
| **Faster Speed**      | - Light signals travel faster than electrical pulses. <br> - Minimal latency for real-time communication.                            |     |
| **Longer Distances**  | - Low attenuation (signal loss): Signals travel 100+ km without repeaters. <br> - Copper requires frequent signal regeneration.      |     |
| **Immunity to EMI**   | - Glass/plastic core is unaffected by electromagnetic interference (e.g., motors, power lines). <br> - Copper is prone to noise.     |     |
| **Security**          | - Difficult to tap without detection. <br> - Copper emits detectable electromagnetic signals.                                        |     |
| **Thinner & Lighter** | - Easier to install in tight spaces. <br> - Reduces cable bulk in data centers.                                                      |     |
| **Durability**        | - Resists temperature fluctuations, corrosion, and moisture.                                                                         |     |

### **3. Guided Media Connectors**  
Connectors link guided media (cables) to devices, ensuring stable communication.  

#### **Common Types**  
| **Connector** | **Used For**         | **Example Applications**                          |  
|---------------|----------------------|--------------------------------------------------|  
| **RJ-45**     | Twisted Pair (UTP/STP)| Ethernet LANs, telephones.                        |  
| **BNC**       | Coaxial Cable        | CCTV systems, older Ethernet networks.           |  
| **SC/LC**     | Fiber Optic Cable    | High-speed data centers, telecom networks.       |  

#### **Advantages of Guided Media Connectors**  
- **Reliability**: Ensure stable physical connections (e.g., RJ-45 for secure LAN links).  
- **Standardization**: Universally compatible (e.g., SC connectors for fiber optics).  
- **Durability**: Designed to withstand frequent plugging/unplugging (e.g., BNC in industrial settings).  

#### **Disadvantages**  
- **Cost**: Fiber connectors (e.g., LC) are expensive compared to RJ-45.  
- **Complex Installation**: Fiber connectors require precise alignment and polishing.  
- **Fragility**: Delicate components (e.g., fiber optic ferrules) can break if mishandled.  

---

### **Key Takeaways**  
- **Fiber Optic Elements**: Core (transmits light), cladding (reflects light), jacket (protection).  
- **Fiber vs. Copper**: Fiber excels in speed, distance, security, and EMI resistance.  
- **Connectors**: Choose based on media type (RJ-45 for UTP, SC/LC for fiber). Pros: reliability; Cons: cost and complexity.  

**Example Use Case**:  
- **Data Center**: Uses LC fiber connectors for high-speed backbone links.  
- **Home Network**: Uses RJ-45 connectors with UTP cables for cost-effective connectivity.

### **3. Unguided (Wireless) Media**  

#### **Radio Waves**  
- **Frequency**: 3 kHz – 1 GHz.  
- **Characteristics**: Omnidirectional, penetrates walls.  
- **Applications**: FM radio, TV broadcasting, mobile networks.  
- **Advantages**: Wide coverage, multicast support.  
- **Disadvantages**: Susceptible to interference.  

#### **Microwaves**  
- **Frequency**: 1 GHz – 1000 GHz.  
  - **Terrestrial**:  
    - Line-of-sight transmission between ground antennas.  
    - **Applications**: Short-distance communication (e.g., cellular towers).  
  - **Satellite**:  
    - Signals relayed via satellites.  
    - **Applications**: GPS, TV broadcasting, global communication.  
- **Advantages**: Long-distance, no cables required.  
- **Disadvantages**: Weather-dependent, high infrastructure cost.  

#### **Infrared**  
- **Frequency**: 300 GHz – 400 THz.  
- **Characteristics**: Short-range, cannot penetrate walls.  
- **Applications**: TV remotes, device pairing (e.g., phone to laptop).  
- **Advantages**: Secure, high bandwidth.  
- **Disadvantages**: Limited to line-of-sight.

### **4. Key Factors in Transmission Media Selection**  
| **Factor**          | **Description**                                                                 |  
|----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|  
| **Bandwidth**        | Higher bandwidth → higher data rates (e.g., fiber > coaxial > UTP).             |  
| **Attenuation**      | Signal loss over distance (fiber has the least attenuation).                     |  
| **Cost**             | Fiber is costly to install; UTP is economical for short distances.              |  
| **EMI Resistance**   | Fiber is immune to EMI; twisted pair/coaxial require shielding.                  |  
| **Installation**     | Wireless media (microwave) avoid physical infrastructure but face signal issues. |  

---

### **5. Applications of Transmission Media**  
- **LANs**: UTP (Cat 5/6), fiber optics.  
- **WANs/Internet Backbone**: Fiber optics for high-speed, long-distance.  
- **Telecom**: Coaxial for TV, fiber for high-speed internet.  
- **Wireless Networks**: Radio waves (Wi-Fi), microwaves (satellite links).  

---

### **6. Advantages vs. Disadvantages**  
| **Media**          | **Advantages**                              | **Disadvantages**                          |  
|---------------------|--------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|  
| **UTP**            | Cheap, easy to install                     | Short range, prone to EMI                  |  
| **Fiber Optics**    | High speed, long-distance, secure          | Expensive, fragile                         |  
| **Microwave**       | No cables, long-distance                   | Weather-dependent, high cost               |  
| **Infrared**        | Secure, high bandwidth                     | Line-of-sight, short range                 |  

---

### **7. Key Terms**  
- **Attenuation**: Signal loss over distance.  
- **EMI (Electromagnetic Interference)**: Disruption caused by external signals.  
- **Refractive Index**: Property determining light reflection in fiber optics.  
- **Line-of-Sight**: Direct path required for transmission (e.g., microwaves).
