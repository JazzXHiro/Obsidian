# Ethernet & Gigabit Ethernet(GBE)

**Study Notes: Ethernet and Gigabit Ethernet (GbE)**

---

### **1. Ethernet Overview**  
- **Definition**: A family of networking technologies for wired LANs, standardized by IEEE 802.3.  
- **Key Features**:  
  - **Speed**: Originally 10 Mbps, later evolved to 100 Mbps (Fast Ethernet).  
  - **Media**: Uses twisted pair (Cat3/Cat5), coaxial, or fiber optic cables.  
  - **Topology**: Typically star topology with switches/hubs.  
  - **Access Method**: CSMA/CD (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection) for half-duplex communication.  

#### **Common Ethernet Standards**  
| **Standard**   | **Speed** | **Media**             | **Max Distance** |  
|----------------|-----------|-----------------------|------------------|  
| **10BASE-T**   | 10 Mbps   | Cat3/Cat5 UTP         | 100 meters       |  
| **100BASE-TX** | 100 Mbps  | Cat5 UTP              | 100 meters       |  
| **100BASE-FX** | 100 Mbps  | Multi-mode fiber      | 2 km             |  

---

### **2. Gigabit Ethernet (GbE)**  
- **Definition**: An Ethernet standard supporting 1 Gbps (1000 Mbps) data rates, defined under IEEE 802.3z (fiber) and 802.3ab (copper).  
- **Key Features**:  
  - **Speed**: 1 Gbps, backward compatible with 10/100 Mbps Ethernet.  
  - **Media**:  
    - **Copper**: Cat5e/Cat6 UTP (1000BASE-T).  
    - **Fiber**: 1000BASE-SX (short wavelength) / 1000BASE-LX (long wavelength).  
  - **Full-Duplex**: Eliminates collisions (no CSMA/CD required).  
  - **Jumbo Frames**: Supports larger payloads (up to 9000 bytes) for efficiency.  

#### **Gigabit Ethernet Standards**  
| **Standard**    | **Speed** | **Media**             | **Max Distance** |  
|------------------|-----------|-----------------------|------------------|  
| **1000BASE-T**   | 1 Gbps    | Cat5e/Cat6 UTP        | 100 meters       |  
| **1000BASE-SX**  | 1 Gbps    | Multi-mode fiber      | 550 meters       |  
| **1000BASE-LX**  | 1 Gbps    | Single-mode fiber     | 5 km             |  

---

### **3. Key Differences: Ethernet vs. Gigabit Ethernet**  
| **Feature**          | **Ethernet**                   | **Gigabit Ethernet**              |  
|----------------------|--------------------------------|-----------------------------------|  
| **Speed**            | 10/100 Mbps                   | 1 Gbps                            |  
| **Duplex Mode**      | Half-duplex (CSMA/CD)         | Full-duplex (no CSMA/CD)          |  
| **Cable Requirements** | Cat3/Cat5                   | Cat5e/Cat6 or fiber               |  
| **Latency**          | Higher                        | Lower                             |  
| **Applications**     | Basic data transfer           | High-bandwidth tasks (video, cloud)|  

---

### **4. Advantages of Gigabit Ethernet**  
- **High Bandwidth**: Supports data-intensive applications (e.g., 4K video, large file transfers).  
- **Scalability**: Backward compatibility with older Ethernet devices.  
- **Efficiency**: Full-duplex mode doubles effective bandwidth (2 Gbps total).  
- **Future-Proofing**: Foundation for 10 GbE and higher standards.  

---

### **5. Disadvantages of Gigabit Ethernet**  
- **Cost**: Higher cost for advanced cables (Cat6, fiber) and switches.  
- **Cabling Limitations**: Requires high-quality cables (Cat5e or better) for copper.  
- **Power Consumption**: More power-hungry than older Ethernet standards.  

---

### **6. Applications**  
- **Ethernet**:  
  - Small office/home networks.  
  - Printers, IoT devices, legacy systems.  
- **Gigabit Ethernet**:  
  - Data centers and server farms.  
  - Video editing/streaming, cloud computing.  
  - High-performance computing (HPC).  

---

### **7. Evolution Beyond GbE**  
- **10 Gigabit Ethernet (10GbE)**: 10 Gbps speeds for enterprise networks.  
- **25/40/100 Gigabit Ethernet**: Used in hyperscale data centers and backbone networks.  

--- 

**Key Terms**:  
- **CSMA/CD**: Manages data collisions in half-duplex Ethernet.  
- **Jumbo Frames**: Larger payloads reduce overhead in GbE.  
- **Full-Duplex**: Simultaneous two-way communication (no collisions).  

**Summary**:  
Ethernet forms the backbone of LANs, while Gigabit Ethernet addresses modern high-speed demands. GbE’s full-duplex mode, fiber support, and backward compatibility make it ideal for today’s data-heavy environments.
