# 12. Quality of Service

### 📌 **1. What is QoS?**

**Quality of Service (QoS)** refers to a set of technologies and techniques used to ensure **reliable, efficient, and predictable performance** of a network.

QoS is important for **real-time applications** like:

- Video conferencing
    
- VoIP (Voice over IP)
    
- Online gaming
    
- Streaming media
    

---

### 🎯 **2. Objectives of QoS**

- **Minimize delay (latency)**
    
- **Maximize bandwidth utilization**
    
- **Avoid packet loss**
    
- **Ensure jitter-free delivery**
    
- Provide **priority** to critical traffic
    

---

### 🔧 **3. Key QoS Parameters**

|Parameter|Description|
|---|---|
|**Bandwidth**|Maximum data rate of a link (measured in bps)|
|**Delay**|Time taken for a packet to travel from source to destination|
|**Jitter**|Variation in packet arrival time|
|**Packet Loss**|Percentage of packets lost during transmission|
|**Reliability**|Ability of the network to deliver packets accurately and on time|

---

### 📦 **4. QoS Techniques**

#### A. **Traffic Classification and Marking**

- Identifying and tagging packets based on priority.
    
- Tools: DSCP (Differentiated Services Code Point), IP precedence.
    

#### B. **Traffic Shaping**

- **Controls the rate of outgoing traffic**.
    
- Smoothens bursty traffic.
    
- Examples: **Leaky Bucket**, **Token Bucket** algorithms.
    

#### C. **Policing**

- Drops or marks packets that exceed the traffic limits.
    

#### D. **Queuing Techniques**

- **FIFO (First-In, First-Out)** – simple but no prioritization.
    
- **Priority Queuing (PQ)** – high-priority traffic is served first.
    
- **Weighted Fair Queuing (WFQ)** – assigns bandwidth proportionally to traffic classes.
    

#### E. **Scheduling**

- Determines the order in which packets are transmitted.
    
- Example: Round Robin, Fair Queuing.
    

---

### 🗂️ **5. QoS Models**

|Model|Description|
|---|---|
|**Best Effort**|No guarantees, all traffic treated equally (default)|
|**Integrated Services (IntServ)**|Provides strict QoS guarantees via resource reservation (uses RSVP)|
|**Differentiated Services (DiffServ)**|Classifies and manages traffic using DSCP; scalable|

---

### ⚖️ **6. IntServ vs DiffServ**

|Feature|IntServ|DiffServ|
|---|---|---|
|Scalability|Not scalable (per-flow state)|Highly scalable (per-class)|
|Signaling|Uses RSVP for resource reservation|No signaling, uses marking|
|Guarantee|Provides strict guarantees|Provides relative priority|

---

### 🔄 **7. Why QoS is Needed?**

- To **support critical real-time applications**
    
- To **avoid congestion** and packet loss
    
- To **prioritize** business or time-sensitive traffic
    
- To **ensure user satisfaction**
