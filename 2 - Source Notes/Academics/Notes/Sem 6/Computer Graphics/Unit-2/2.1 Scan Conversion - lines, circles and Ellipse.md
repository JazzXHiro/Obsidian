# Scan Conversion - lines, circles and Ellipse

## Line-Drawing Algorithms

A straight-line segment in a scene is defined by the coordinate positions for the
endpoints of the segment. To display the line on a raster monitor, the graphics system must first project the endpoints to integer screen coordinates and determine the nearest pixel positions along the line path between the two end points. Then the line color is loaded into the frame buffer at the corresponding pixel coordinates.

Reading from the frame buffer, the video controller plots the screen pixels. This process digitizes the line into a set of discrete integer positions that, in general, only approximates the actual line path. Acomputed line position of (10.48, 20.51), for example, is converted to pixel position (10, 21). This rounding of coordinate values to integers causes all but horizontal and vertical lines to be displayed with a stair-step appearance (known as “the jaggies”)

#### Line Equations

The cartesian slope-intercept equation for a straight line -

$$y=mx+b$$

with $m$ as the slope of the line and $b$ as the $y$ intercept. Given that two end points of a line segment are specified at positions $(x_{0}, y_{{0}})$ and $(x_{end}, y_{end}).$
$$\begin{align}
m=\left( \frac{{y_{end} - y_{0}}}{x_{end} - x_{0}} \right) \\ \\
b = y_{0} - m.x_{0}
\end{align}$$

### Digital Differential Algorithm (DDA)

The Digital Differential Algorithm is used to draw a straight line between two given points in computer graphics.

> It works by calculating intermediate points using the slope of the line.

**Given:**

Let the endpoints be:

$$
\begin{align}
(x_1, y_1) \quad \text{and} \quad (x_2, y_2) \\
\end{align}
$$ 
$$
\begin{align}
\Delta x = x_2 - x_1 \\
\Delta y = y_2 - y_1  
\end{align}
$$

**Slope:**

$$
m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}
$$

#### Algorithm Logic -

**Case 1: |m| < 1 (Line is less steep)**

Increment $x$ by $1$ and compute corresponding $y$.

$$\begin{align}
x_{i+1} = x_{i} + 1 \\
y_{i+1} = y_{i} + m
\end{align}$$


> [!NOTE] Why the + 1 in $x_{i}$ in above equation
> Because pixels exist at integer grid positions.
> 
> On the screen
> - We can not draw at $x=2.3$
> - We can not draw at $x=4.87$
>   
>   We can only draw at :
> $$(2,3),(3,3),(4,3)\dots$$
> 
> So we move one pixel at a time.
> 
> If slope is small:
> $$|m| < 1$$
> That means:
> $$Δx>Δy$$
> So the lines move more horizontally than vertically.
> 
> Therefore:
> - We step forward one pixel in x each iteration
> - Then calculate corresponding y

**Case 2: |m| $\geq$ 1 (Line is steep)**

Increment $y$ by $1$ and compute corresponding $x.$

$$\begin{align}
y_{i+1} = y_{i} + 1 \\
x_{i+1}=x_{i}+\frac{1}{m}
\end{align}$$

#### Steps of DDA Algorithm

1. Input $x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2}$
2. Compute:
	    $\Delta x=x2−x1$
	    $\Delta y=y_{2} - y_{1}$
3. Calculate number of steps:
$$steps=max(|\Delta x|,|\Delta y|)$$
4. Compute increments:
	   $$\begin{align}
	x_{inc}= \frac{\Delta x}{steps} \\
	y_{inc}=\frac{\Delta y}{steps}
	\end{align}$$
5. Start from $(x_{1},y_{1}).$
6. Plot next point using:
	   $$\begin{align}
		x=x+x_{inc} \\
		y=y+{y_{inc}}
		\end{align}$$
#### Example

1.
$$
\begin{align}
(x_1, y_1) = (1,1) \\
(x_2, y_2) = (7,4)  
\end{align}
$$
Calculate $\Delta x \ \text{and} \ \Delta y,$

$$\Delta x = x_2 - x_1 = 7 - 1 = 6$$
$$\Delta y = y_2 - y_1 = 4 - 1 = 3$$

Calculate Slope,

$$
\begin{align}
m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} \\
m = \frac{3}{6} = 0.5
\end{align}
$$


Since:

$$|m| < 1$$
The line is **shallow**  
We increment **x by 1** each step.

For (|m| < 1):

$$x_{i+1} = x_i + 1$$
$$y_{i+1} = y_i + m$$$$m = 0.5$$

So:

$$y_{i+1} = y_i + 0.5$$
##### Iteration Table

| Step | x   | y (calculated) |
| ---- | --- | -------------- |
| 1    | 1   | 1              |
| 2    | 2   | 1 + 0.5 = 1.5  |
| 3    | 3   | 1.5 + 0.5 = 2  |
| 4    | 4   | 2 + 0.5 = 2.5  |
| 5    | 5   | 2.5 + 0.5 = 3  |
| 6    | 6   | 3 + 0.5 = 3.5  |
| 7    | 7   | 3.5 + 0.5 = 4  |

---

##### Points Generated (Before Rounding)

(1,1), (2,1.5), (3,2), (4,2.5), (5,3), (6,3.5), (7,4)

---

##### Pixel Points (After Rounding)

Since pixels must be integers, round y values:

(1,1), (2,2), (3,2), (4,3), (5,3), (6,4), (7,4)

---

##### Why This Works

Because:

$$
\begin{align}
m = \frac{y_{i+1} - y_i}{x_{i+1} - x_i} \\ \\
\text{Since, }x_{i+1} = x_{i} + 1   \\ \\
m = \frac{y_{i+1} - y_i}{x_{i}+1-x_{i}} \\ \\
m = \frac{y_{i+1} - y_i}{1} \\ \\
y_{i+1} - y_i = m   \\ \\
y_{i+1} = y_i + m
\end{align}
$$
---
#### Limitations of DDA

- Uses floating point arithmetic
- Slower compared to Bresenham's algorithm.
- Rounding errors may occur.

### Bresenham's Line Drawing Algorithm

#### Overview
Bresenham's algorithm is an efficient line drawing algorithm that uses integer arithmetic only, making it faster than DDA.

##### Given
Endpoints:
$$
\begin{align}
(x_{1}, y_{1}, (x_{2},y_{2})) \\ \\
\Delta x=x_{2}-x_{1} \\
\Delta y=y_{2}-y_{1}
\end{align}
$$
Slope:
$$
\begin{align}
m=\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}
\end{align}
$$
##### Decision Parameter
Initial decision parameter:
$$
\begin{align}
P_{0}=2\Delta y-\Delta x
\end{align}
$$

###### Case 1: m < 1
**If $P_{k}<0$**
$$
\begin{align}
x_{k+1} &=x_{k}+1 \\
y_{k+1}&=y_{k} \\
P_{k+1}&=P_{k}+2\Delta y
\end{align}
$$
**If $P_{k}\geq0$
$$
\begin{align}
x_{k+1} &=x_{k}+1 \\
y_{k+1}&=y_{k} +1 \\
P_{k+1}&=P_{k}+2\Delta y - 2\Delta x
\end{align}
$$

###### Case 2: m > 1
**If $P_{k}<0$
$$
\begin{align}
x_{k+1} &=x_{k} \\
y_{k+1}&=y_{k} + 1\\
P_{k+1}&=P_{k}+2\Delta x
\end{align}
$$
If $P_{k}\geq0$
$$
\begin{align}
x_{k+1} &=x_{k}+1 \\
y_{k+1}&=y_{k} +1 \\
P_{k+1}&=P_{k}+2\Delta x - 2\Delta y
\end{align}
$$

#### Steps of Bresenham's Algorithm

1. Input endpoints.

2. Compute Δx\Delta xΔx and Δy\Delta yΔy.

3. Initialize:
	$$P_{0}=2Δy−Δx$$
4. Repeat until x=x2x = x_2x=x2​:
    
    - If Pk<0P_→ choose East pixel
        
    - Else → choose North-East pixel
        
- Update decision parameter accordingly.
