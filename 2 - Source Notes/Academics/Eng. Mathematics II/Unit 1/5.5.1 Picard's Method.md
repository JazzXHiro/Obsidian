# Picard's Method

# 📘 Picard Method of Successive Approximation

## Problem:

Solve the differential equation using Picard Method (up to third approximation):

$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = x + y^2
$$

Given:
- $y(0) = 0$
- Find $y(0.1)$

---

## General Picard Formula:

$$
y_{n+1} = y_0 + \int_{x_0}^{x} f(x, y_n) \, dx
$$

---

## Step-by-step Approximation:

### Step 0 (Initial):
Let $x_0 = 0$, $y_0 = 0$

### First Approximation ($n = 0$):
$$
y_1 = y_0 + \int_{0}^{x} (x + 0^2) \, dx = \int_{0}^{x} x \, dx = \frac{x^2}{2}
$$

---

### Second Approximation ($n = 1$):
$$
y_2 = y_0 + \int_{0}^{x} (x + y_1^2) \, dx
= \int_{0}^{x} \left(x + \left(\frac{x^2}{2}\right)^2 \right) dx
= \int_{0}^{x} \left(x + \frac{x^4}{4} \right) dx
= \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{x^5}{20}
$$

---

### Third Approximation ($n = 2$):

$$
y_3 = y_0 + \int_{0}^{x} \left( x + y_2^2 \right) dx
= \int_0^x \left(x + \left( \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{x^5}{20} \right)^2 \right) dx
$$

Expanded (approximated):
$$
y_3 = \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{5x^5}{20} + \frac{8x^8}{160} + \frac{11x^{11}}{44\omega}
$$

---

## Final Substitution:

At $x = 0.1$:

$$
y_3(0.1) = \frac{(0.1)^2}{2} + \frac{(0.1)^5}{20} + \frac{(0.1)^8}{160} + \frac{(0.1)^{11}}{44\omega}
$$
