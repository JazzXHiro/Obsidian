# Finite Difference Operators

# Numerical Operator and Their Relation

## $1.$ Forward Difference Operator:

Let $y = f(x)$ be a function where the argument $x$ can take the equidistant values  
$$a,\ a+h,\ a+2h,\ \dots,\ a+nh$$  
and $f(a),\ f(a+h),\ \dots,\ f(a+nh)$ are values of the given points.


> [!NOTE] Note
> h is the step size here.



---

### Definition:

The **forward difference operator** is defined as:

$$
\Delta f(x) = f(x + h) - f(x)
$$

---

### Higher Order Differences:

The **second order forward difference** is given by:

$$
\Delta^2 f(x) = \Delta\left[f(x + h) - f(x)\right] = f(x + 2h) - 2f(x + h) + f(x)
$$

---

### Examples:

- $\Delta f(a) = f(a + h) - f(a)$
- $\Delta f(a + h) = f(a + 2h) - f(a + h)$
- $\Delta f(a + 2h) = f(a + 3h) - f(a + 2h)$

---

### In General:

$$
\Delta f(x) = f(x + h) - f(x)
$$

$$
\Delta^2 f(x) = \Delta f(x + h) - \Delta f(x)
$$

Which simplifies to:

$$
\Delta^2 f(x) = f(x + 2h) - 2f(x + h) + f(x)
$$


---
# Finite Difference Operator – Important Notes

> 💡 **Note**:  
> $\Delta^2$ is **not** the square of the operator $\Delta$.  
> It means $\Delta$ operated by $\Delta$:
> $$
\Delta^2 = \Delta(\Delta)
$$

---

## Fundamental Theorem of Finite Differences

If  
$$
f(x) = a_0x^n + a_1x^{n-1} + a_2x^{n-2} + \dots + a_n
$$  
is an $n$-degree polynomial and $a_0 \neq 0$, then:
$$
\Delta^n f(x) = a_0 \cdot n! \cdot h^n
$$

---

## Example

Evaluate:
$$
\Delta^6 \left[(ax - 1)(bx^2 - 1)(cx^3 - 1)\right], \quad \text{where } h = 1
$$

### Solution:

First, expand the expression:
$$
\Delta^6 \left[ abcx^6 - bcx^5 - acx^4 + (ab + c)x^3 + bx^2 + ax - 1 \right]
$$

Now apply the rule:
$$
\Delta^6 f(x) = abc \cdot 6! \cdot h^6
$$

Since $h = 1$:
$$
= abc \cdot 720
$$

---

## Special Case:

If the degree of the polynomial is less than the order of the difference operator, the result is:
$$
\Delta^n f(x) = 0
$$  
For example:
$$
\Delta^2(x) = 0
$$  
since $x$ is a first-degree polynomial and $\Delta^2$ is a second-order operator.
