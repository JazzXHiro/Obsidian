# Bisection Method

## Bisection Method -

This is based on the repeated application of intermediate value property. Let $f(x)$ be continuous between $a$ & $b$ -

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Let } f(a) \text{ be -ve.} \\
\text{Then } f(b) \text{ is +ve.}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then the first approximation of the root is $x_{1} = \frac{a+b}{2}$, $\text{if } f(x_{1}=0)$, then it is the root at $f(x) = 0$.

**Otherwise,**

The root lies between $a$ & $x_1$, or $x_1$ & $b$ according as $f(x_1)$ is positive or negative.
Then we bisect the interval as before & continue the process until the root is found accurately.


### Solve

$$
x^2 - 2x - 7 = 0
$$

**on the interval $[3,4]$:**

1. Check endpoints:  
   $f(3)=9-6-7=-4<0$,  $f(4)=16-8-7=1>0$  
2. **Iteration 1:**  
   - $x_1=(3+4)/2=3.5$  
   - $f(3.5)=12.25-7-7=-1.75<0$  
   - New bracket: $[3.5,\,4]$  
3. **Iteration 2:**  
   - $x_2=(3.5+4)/2=3.75$  
   - $f(3.75)=14.0625-7.5-7=-0.4375<0$  
   - New bracket: $[3.75,\,4]$  

### 🔍 Example: Find the Real Root using Bisection Method

Given the equation:  
$$
f(x) = x^3 - x - 1 = 0
$$

---

#### ➤ Step 1: Initial Interval

Try values of $x$ to find a sign change:

- $f(-1) = (-1)^3 - (-1) - 1 = -1$
- $f(0) = 0^3 - 0 - 1 = -1$
- $f(1) = 1^3 - 1 - 1 = -1$
- $f(2) = 8 - 2 - 1 = 5$

Since $f(1) < 0$ and $f(2) > 0$, a root lies between $1$ and $2$.

---

#### ➤ Step 2: First Approximation

$$
x_1 = \frac{1 + 2}{2} = 1.5
$$

Evaluate:

$$
f(1.5) = (1.5)^3 - 1.5 - 1 = 3.375 - 1.5 - 1 = 0.875 = \frac{7}{8}
$$

Since $f(1.5) > 0$, update the interval to $[1, 1.5]$

---

#### ➤ Step 3: Second Approximation

$$
x_2 = \frac{1 + 1.5}{2} = 1.25
$$

Evaluate:

$$
f(1.25) = (1.25)^3 - 1.25 - 1 = \frac{125}{64} - 1.25 - 1 = \frac{-19}{64}
$$

$f(1.25) < 0$, so the root lies between $1.25$ and $1.5$

---

#### ➤ Step 4: Third Approximation

$$
x_3 = \frac{1.25 + 1.5}{2} = 1.375
$$

Evaluate:

- $f(1.375) > 0$  
- Update interval: $[1.25, 1.375]$

---

#### ➤ Step 5: Fourth Approximation

$$
x_4 = \frac{1.25 + 1.375}{2} = 1.3125
$$

The method continues this way until the desired accuracy is achieved.

---

#### 📈 Note:

Each step halves the interval, improving the approximation of the root of $f(x) = 0$.

### Bisection Method: Solving $x^3 - 2x - 5 = 0$ to Three‐Decimal Accuracy

#### 1. Problem Statement  
Find the root of  
$$
x^3 - 2x - 5 = 0
$$  
using the bisection method, accurate to the third decimal place.

---

#### 2. Bracketing the Root  
1. Choose initial endpoints $a=2$ and $b=3$.  
2. Compute  
   - $f(2) = 2^3 - 2\cdot2 -5 = -1$  
   - $f(3) = 3^3 - 2\cdot3 -5 = 16$  
3. Since $f(2)\cdot f(3) < 0$, there is at least one root in $[2,3]$.

---

#### 3. Number of Iterations Required  (Optional)
We need the final interval width $\le 0.001$ (so the midpoint rounded to three decimals is correct).  
Starting width $= b - a = 1$. After $n$ bisections, width $= 1/2^n$. We require  
$$
\frac{1}{2^n} \le 0.001 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2^n \ge 1000 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad n \ge \log_2(1000) \approx 9.97.
$$  
Thus **10 iterations** suffice.

---

#### 4. Iterative Procedure

---

#### 5. Final Approximation  
After 10 iterations the interval is  
$$
[\,2.09375,\;2.09570\,]
$$  
with midpoint  
$$
c_{10} = 2.0947265625.
$$  
Rounding to three decimal places gives  
$$
\boxed{x \approx 2.095}
$$  
which is the root of $x^3 - 2x - 5 = 0$ accurate to $10^{-3}$.
