# 3. Internet Protocol

- **Definition**:  
  - A 32-bit address uniquely identifying a device's connection to the Internet (e.g., computers, routers).  

- **Length**:  
  - 32 bits, typically represented in **dotted-decimal notation** (e.g., `192.168.1.1`).  
  - Divided into 4 octets (8 bits each), ranging from `0.0.0.0` to `255.255.255.255`.  

- **Uniqueness**:  
  - No two devices can share the same public IPv4 address **simultaneously** (ensures proper routing).  
  - Exceptions: Private networks use NAT (Network Address Translation) to share a single public IP.  

- **Address Space**:  
  - Total possible addresses: \(2^{32} = 4,294,967,296\) (over 4 billion).  
  - **Limitations**: Exhaustion due to reserved blocks, inefficient allocation, and Internet growth.  

- **Key Context**:  
  - IPv4 is being phased out in favor of **IPv6** (128-bit address space) to accommodate more devices.  

---  
**Summary**: IPv4 addresses are 32-bit unique identifiers for devices on the Internet, with a finite pool of ~4.3 billion addresses. Limitations led to workarounds (e.g., NAT) and the adoption of IPv6.
